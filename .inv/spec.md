## 1. Executive Summary & Objective

The objective of the **`.inv`** sub-project is to design, implement, and integrate an automatic, real-time 3D potential field inversion system into the **Mod3D** software ecosystem. 

Building upon the legacy research of **`Inv3D`** (*Cerovský*), the mathematical rigor of **Reweighted Focusing Inversion** (*Portniaguine & Zhdanov, 1999; Zhdanov, 2002*), and modern breakthroughs in **3D Earth System & Weather Transformers** (*Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, ClimaX*), `.inv` delivers:
1. **Instantaneous 3D Inversion ($< 100\text{ ms}$)** of surface gravity and magnetic field data into 3D voxel density and susceptibility volumes.
2. **Sharp, Geologically Realistic Boundaries** via physics-informed Minimum Support ($S_{MS}$) and Minimum Gradient Support ($S_{MGS}$) regularizers that eliminate diffuse Tikhonov artifacts.
3. **Complete Independence of the Computational Core**, mirroring the decoupled architecture of `pfld` (pure C++20, header-only, zero dependencies on Qt or Mod3D GUI).
4. **Seamless Bidirectional Integration with Mod3D**, allowing voxel models to be converted into Mod3D polyhedral bodies/column grids and vice versa.

## 2. Mathematical & Physical Formulation

### 2.1 The Forward Potential Field Operator

Let the subsurface modeling domain $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be discretized into a regular grid of $M = N_x \times N_y \times N_z$ cuboid cells (voxels), where each cell $V_j$ occupies the spatial bounds:
$$V_j = [x_{1,j}, x_{2,j}] \times [y_{1,j}, y_{2,j}] \times [z_{1,j}, z_{2,j}]$$

Let $m \in \mathbb{R}^M$ denote the subsurface property vector (density contrast $\Delta \rho$ in $\text{g/cm}^3$ or magnetic susceptibility $\chi$ in SI units).

For an observation point $\mathbf{r}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)$ on or above the topographic surface, the vertical component of the gravitational attraction $\Delta g(\mathbf{r}_i)$ generated by cell $V_j$ with uniform density contrast $\Delta \rho_j$ is given by Newton's law of gravitation:
$$\Delta g(\mathbf{r}_i) = \gamma \Delta \rho_j \int_{V_j} \frac{z' - z_i}{\|\mathbf{r}' - \mathbf{r}_i\|^3} \, dx' dy' dz'$$
where $\gamma = 6.67430 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{m}^3 \text{kg}^{-1} \text{s}^{-2}$ is the universal gravitational constant.

#### Exact Analytical Prism Kernel (*Nagy, 1966*)
Carrying out the volume integral over the right rectangular prism yields the exact closed-form expression:
$$\Delta g(\mathbf{r}_i) = \gamma \Delta \rho_j \sum_{p=1}^2 \sum_{q=1}^2 \sum_{s=1}^2 (-1)^{p+q+s} \left[ \Delta x_p \ln(\Delta y_q + R) + \Delta y_q \ln(\Delta x_p + R) - \Delta z_s \arctan\left(\frac{\Delta x_p \Delta y_q}{\Delta z_s R}\right) \right]$$
where:
$$\Delta x_p = x_{p,j} - x_i, \quad \Delta y_q = y_{q,j} - y_i, \quad \Delta z_s = z_{s,j} - z_i, \quad R = \sqrt{\Delta x_p^2 + \Delta y_q^2 + \Delta z_s^2}$$

In matrix-vector notation, the discrete linear forward mapping for $N$ observations is:
$$\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{G} \mathbf{m} + \boldsymbol{\eta}$$
where $\mathbf{G} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}$ is the sensitivity (kernel) matrix, and $\boldsymbol{\eta} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$ represents observational noise.

### 2.2 Ill-Posedness & Depth Weighting

Because $\mathbf{G}$ decays as $1/r^2$ (for gravity) and $1/r^3$ (for magnetic fields and gravity gradients), surface observations are predominantly sensitive to shallow structures. Unconstrained inversions inevitably place anomalous masses directly at the surface.

To counteract this decay, classical inversion (*Li & Oldenburg, 1996, 1998*) applies a depth weighting matrix $\mathbf{W}_z = \text{diag}(w_j)$:
$$w_j = \frac{1}{(z_j + z_0)^{\beta / 2}}$$
where $z_j$ is the depth to the center of cell $j$, $z_0$ is a small parameter preventing singularity at $z=0$, and $\beta = 2$ for gravity ($\beta = 3$ for magnetics).

---

### 2.3 Reweighted Focusing Inversion (*Zhdanov & Portniaguine*)

Traditional Tikhonov $L_2$ regularization minimizes:
$$\Phi(m) = \|\mathbf{W}_d (\mathbf{G}\mathbf{m} - \mathbf{d})\|_2^2 + \alpha \|\mathbf{W}_m (\mathbf{m} - \mathbf{m}_{prior})\|_2^2$$
This produces smooth, blurry density/susceptibility clouds, failing to locate true lithological boundaries, fault scarps, or compact mineralized bodies.

**Portniaguine & Zhdanov (1999, 2002)** introduced **Focusing Stabilizers** designed to minimize the geometrical volume of anomalous sources and maximize boundary sharpness:

#### 1. Minimum Support (MS) Stabilizer
$$S_{MS}(\mathbf{m}) = \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{(m_j - m_{0,j})^2}{(m_j - m_{0,j})^2 + e^2}$$
* When $|m_j - m_{0,j}| \gg e$, the term approaches $1$ (acting as a count of non-zero anomalous cells, approximating an $L_0$ pseudo-norm).
* When $|m_j - m_{0,j}| \ll e$, the term smoothly vanishes, suppressing background noise.
* Minimizing $S_{MS}(\mathbf{m})$ forces the inverted anomaly into a **compact, focused volume**.

#### 2. Minimum Gradient Support (MGS) Stabilizer
$$S_{MGS}(\mathbf{m}) = \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{\|\nabla m_j\|^2}{\|\nabla m_j\|^2 + e_{grad}^2}$$
* Minimizes the spatial area over which model property changes occur.
* Penalizes smooth gradient ramps, yielding **sharp, piecewise-constant blocky boundaries**.

#### 3. Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) Formulation
Both stabilizers are cast into iteratively reweighted forms:
$$\mathbf{W}_e^{(k)} = \text{diag}\left( \frac{1}{\left( (m_j^{(k-1)} - m_{0,j})^2 + e^2 \right)^{1/2}} \right)$$
At each iteration $k$, the solution updates via:
$$\mathbf{m}^{(k)} = \left( \mathbf{G}^T \mathbf{W}_d^T \mathbf{W}_d \mathbf{G} + \alpha (\mathbf{W}_e^{(k)})^T \mathbf{W}_e^{(k)} \right)^{-1} \mathbf{G}^T \mathbf{W}_d^T \mathbf{W}_d \mathbf{d}$$
The focusing parameter $e$ is gradually **annealed** ($e_0 \to e_{min}$) over iterations, transitioning from global stability to razor-sharp focusing.

### 3.1 Limitations of Traditional CNNs (3D U-Nets)
1. **Receptive Field Bottleneck**: Convolutional kernels operate locally ($3 \times 3 \times 3$). To propagate signal from a distant surface anomaly to deep voxel layers requires excessive pooling, losing high frequencies and boundary precision.
2. **Physics Incompatibility**: Potential field physics is governed by non-local Green's functions spanning the entire domain. An isolated anomalous mass exerts an influence across all observation stations.
3. **Rigid Observation Grids**: Standard 2D/3D CNNs demand uniform Cartesian rasters, causing severe aliasing when applied to draped airborne flight lines or rugged mountain topography.

### 3.2 Transformer Mechanisms & Innovations

#### 1. Global Non-Local Multi-Head Attention
Multi-Head Attention dynamically correlates every surface measurement with every voxel query:
$$\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \mathbf{B}_{phys} \right) \mathbf{V}$$
where $\mathbf{B}_{phys} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times N}$ is the **Physics-Informed Distance Bias**:
$$\mathbf{B}_{phys}(j, i) = -\beta \ln\left( 1 + \frac{\|\mathbf{r}_j^{cell} - \mathbf{r}_i^{obs}\|^2}{z_0^2} \right)$$
This embeds Newton's power-law decay directly into the softmax attention weights, preventing unphysical distant activations.

#### 2. Cross-Attention 2D-to-3D Dimension Lifting
Rather than naively unflattening 2D feature maps, `.inv` initializes a 3D query token grid $\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{3D} \times d}$ embedded with 3D spatial coordinates $(x, y, z)$. The query tokens cross-attend to the 2D surface observation tokens $\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{2D} \times d}$, lifting surface measurements into the 3D volume in a coordinate-aware manner.

#### 3. 3D Shifted-Window Attention (3D Swin)
Full all-to-all attention across a high-resolution voxel grid ($128 \times 128 \times 64 = 1,048,576$ cells) would require quadratic memory $\mathcal{O}(M^2) \approx 10^{12}$ floats (impossible on modern GPUs).  
Adapting **3D Swin Transformer** (*Liu et al., 2021; Yu et al., 2025*), attention is computed locally within small 3D cuboid windows (e.g., $8 \times 8 \times 4$), shifting windows between consecutive layers. This achieves:
* **Global receptive field across the entire domain** through hierarchical window shifting.
* **Strict linear computational complexity $\mathcal{O}(M)$**, enabling high-resolution 3D inversion in under 50 ms.

## 4. Deep Network Architecture

The `.inv` neural architecture consists of four distinct stages:

```
[2D Observation Map: Δg, g_zz, ΔT] (H × W × C_in)
                    │
                    ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ 2D Patch Tokenizer     │ -> Patch size: 4 × 4, Token dim: 192
       └────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ 2D-to-3D Cross-Lift    │ -> Queries: 3D regular grid (H/4 × W/4 × D/4)
       │ Attention + B_phys     │    Keys/Values: 2D surface tokens
       └────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ 3D Swin Encoder        │
       │ Stage 1 (192-dim, W=4) │ -> Shifted 3D window self-attention
       │ Stage 2 (384-dim, W=4) │ -> 3D Patch Merging (downsamples x,y)
       │ Stage 3 (768-dim, W=4) │ -> Deep spatial context extraction
       └────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ 3D Swin Decoder        │
       │ Stage 3 (384-dim, W=4) │ -> 3D Patch Expanding
       │ Stage 2 (192-dim, W=4) │ -> Skip connections from encoder
       │ Stage 1 (96-dim, W=4)  │
       └────────────────────────┘
                    │
                    ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ Voxel Regression Head  │
       │ Linear + Bound Scaling │ -> Outputs: Density Δρ(x,y,z) & Susc χ(x,y,z)
       └────────────────────────┘
```

### Layer Dimensions & Hyperparameters

* **Input Grid Resolution ($N_x \times N_y$)**: $128 \times 128$ (or variable via sliding window inference).
* **Target 3D Voxel Grid ($N_x \times N_y \times N_z$)**: $128 \times 128 \times 64$ cells.
* **Window Size in 3D Swin**: $(S_x, S_y, S_z) = (8, 8, 4)$ cells.
* **Number of Attention Heads**: $[4, 8, 16, 8]$ across stages.
* **Latent Feature Dimensions**: $[96, 192, 384, 192]$.
* **Total Parameters**: $\approx 42\text{M}$ parameters (fits comfortably into $170\text{ MB}$ ONNX model).

## 5. Physics-Guided Loss Formulation

Training is guided by a compound objective enforcing both data fidelity and geological focusing:

$$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{data} + \lambda_{MS} \mathcal{L}_{MS} + \lambda_{MGS} \mathcal{L}_{MGS} + \lambda_{depth} \mathcal{L}_{depth} + \lambda_{sparse} \mathcal{L}_{sparse}$$

### 1. Data Misfit Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{data}$)
Evaluates the physical accuracy of the predicted model $\hat{\mathbf{m}}$ by passing it through the exact forward kernel $\mathbf{G}$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{data} = \frac{1}{N} \|\mathbf{W}_d (\mathbf{G} \hat{\mathbf{m}} - \mathbf{d}_{obs})\|_2^2 + \frac{\mu}{N} \|\mathbf{W}_d (\mathbf{G} \hat{\mathbf{m}} - \mathbf{d}_{obs})\|_1$$
*(The hybrid $L_2 + L_1$ Huber-like penalty protects against survey outliers and leveling artifacts).*

### 2. Zhdanov Minimum Support Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{MS}$)
Enforces spatial compactness and suppresses diffuse halos:
$$\mathcal{L}_{MS} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{\hat{m}_j^2}{\hat{m}_j^2 + e^2}$$

### 3. Zhdanov Minimum Gradient Support Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{MGS}$)
Drives sharp, blocky boundaries along geological contacts:
$$\mathcal{L}_{MGS} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M \frac{\|\nabla \hat{m}_j\|^2}{\|\nabla \hat{m}_j\|^2 + e_{grad}^2}$$
where $\nabla \hat{m}_j$ is computed using 3D central spatial differences:
$$\|\nabla \hat{m}_{i,j,k}\|^2 = \left(\frac{\hat{m}_{i+1,j,k} - \hat{m}_{i-1,j,k}}{2\Delta x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\hat{m}_{i,j+1,k} - \hat{m}_{i,j-1,k}}{2\Delta y}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\hat{m}_{i,j,k+1} - \hat{m}_{i,j,k-1}}{2\Delta z}\right)^2$$

### 4. Curriculum Annealing Schedule
To prevent premature convergence to non-physical local minima:
* **Epochs 1–15**: Focus on data misfit $\mathcal{L}_{data}$ and mild smoothness ($\lambda_{MS} = 0, e = 0.5$).
* **Epochs 16–35**: Introduce focusing stabilizers ($\lambda_{MS} = 10^{-2}, \lambda_{MGS} = 10^{-2}$), with $e$ decaying exponentially:
  $$e(t) = e_{min} + (e_0 - e_{min}) \exp(-\kappa t)$$
* **Epochs 36–50**: Fine-tuning with sharp focusing ($e = 10^{-3}, e_{grad} = 10^{-3}$).

## 8. Mod3D Integration Contract

### 8.1 Integration with `mod3d_core`
The `mod3d_core` library accesses `.inv` directly via CMake:
```cmake
add_subdirectory(.inv)
target_link_libraries(mod3d_core PUBLIC inv)
```
Conversion utilities translate between `mod3d::Body` polyhedra and `inv::VoxelGrid<T>`:
1. **Voxelization of Polyhedral Bodies**: Any `mod3d::Body` is discretized onto the voxel grid via point-in-polyhedron ray casting.
2. **Isosurface to Facet Polyhedron**: Inverted density volumes are converted into Mod3D facetized bodies using Marching Cubes, enabling immediate forward modeling in `pfld` and real-time interactive editing.

### 8.2 Integration with `mod3d_gui`
The Mod3D user interface exposes a modern **3D Neural Inversion Panel**:
* **Input Observation Selector**: Chooses between measured gravity, residual gravity, and magnetic anomalies.
* **Voxel Domain Bounds**: Configures cell resolution ($\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$) and bounding box.
* **Focusing Tightness Slider**: Controls the Zhdanov focusing threshold ($e_{min}$).
* **Execution**: Runs inference asynchronously in a background thread (`std::jthread`), updating progress bars and rendering 3D orthogonal slices and isosurfaces in the OpenGL viewport upon completion.

## 9. Verification & Quality Standards

1. **Analytical Kernel Parity**: The Nagy prism analytical kernel in `forward_kernel.hpp` is cross-verified against `pfld`'s facet kernel for a 12-facet cuboid, maintaining agreement within $< 10^{-12} \, \text{mGal}$.
2. **Zhdanov Metric Monotonicity**: Unit tests confirm that $S_{MS}(\mathbf{m})$ and $S_{MGS}(\mathbf{m})$ monotonically decrease as models become more compact and piecewise-constant.
3. **Memory Safety**: Strict value semantics, zero raw owning pointers, and standard layout structs ensuring cache-optimal vectorized execution.
4. **Cross-Platform Compatibility**: Clean builds on macOS (Apple Silicon Metal / CoreML execution providers), Linux (CUDA / TensorRT), and Windows (DirectML).
